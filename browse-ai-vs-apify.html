Browse AI vs Apify: Which Web Data Tool Fits Your Workflow?

Browse AI or Apify? Compare setup, maintenance, pricing predictability, prebuilt scrapers, automation, and technical control to choose the right tool for your workflow.

By Useful Work Tools Editorial DeskPublished and reviewed August 15, 2026

Browse AI and Apify can both turn websites into structured data and automated workflows.

But choosing between them based only on feature lists, advertised entry prices, or the number of URLs you need to process misses the most important difference.

The better question is:

Who needs to own, maintain, and change this workflow after you build it?

Browse AI is generally the better starting point when a non-technical person needs to build and maintain a recurring web-data workflow without managing scraping infrastructure.

Apify becomes more attractive when a suitable prebuilt Actor already exists, a developer needs deeper control, or the workflow requires custom scraping logic and infrastructure.

And volume alone does not decide the winner. Both platforms can handle substantial workloads.

This guide focuses on the practical decision: which one better fits the way you actually work?

The 30-second decision

Start here.

Can a non-technical person own this workflow?

If the person responsible for the workflow needs to create, adjust, and troubleshoot it without writing code, Browse AI is a strong first candidate.

Its point-and-click approach is designed around teaching a robot what to extract or monitor.

If that sounds like your situation, our guide to automating website data into Google Sheets shows what this kind of workflow looks like in practice.

But do not stop the comparison there.

If a mature Apify Actor already exists specifically for the website or job you need, Apify may still be worth testing even if you are not a developer.

Does a good prebuilt Apify Actor already exist?

If yes, Apify may be easier than its developer-focused reputation suggests.

Apify advertises a large marketplace of Actors for specific websites and data-collection jobs. Using an existing Actor can eliminate much of the work involved in building a scraper yourself.

If no suitable Actor exists, ask the next question.

Do you need custom scraping logic or deeper infrastructure control?

If yes, Apify is usually the stronger fit.

Developers can create custom Actors and control scraping logic, browser automation, proxies, storage, APIs, and surrounding infrastructure much more directly.

If no, Browse AI's simpler workflow may be enough.

Do you have a lot of URLs?

Don't use this question by itself.

A large workload does not automatically mean Apify.

Browse AI supports bulk workflows as well. The better decision depends on the shape of the workload, the site's difficulty, maintenance requirements, and actual cost.

“More URLs = Apify” is too simplistic to be useful.

The difference that actually matters

The easiest way to understand these products is to think about what each one puts at the center.

Browse AI is workflow-first

Browse AI is designed around creating a robot, extracting or monitoring information, and sending the result somewhere useful.

A typical workflow might be:

Website → Browse AI robot → scheduled run → structured rows → Google Sheets

The person operating that workflow does not necessarily need to understand browser automation, proxies, scraping frameworks, or infrastructure.

That matters for solo operators and small teams because the person who needs the data can often own the automation too.

Apify is platform- and Actor-first

Apify gives you a platform for running web-data programs called Actors.

You can use an existing Actor from the Apify Store or build your own.

That creates a much larger range of possibilities.

An existing Actor may make a specific extraction job surprisingly simple.

A custom Actor can give a developer substantially more control than a point-and-click robot.

The tradeoff is that the decision becomes more technical: you are not only choosing Apify. You are often choosing which Actor, which implementation, and who will maintain it.

Who fixes it when the website changes?

This is one of the most important differences — and one that feature comparison tables rarely discuss.

Websites change.

Buttons move. Page layouts change. Selectors break. Login flows get redesigned. Anti-bot systems evolve.

The question is not whether maintenance will ever happen.

The question is:

Who owns that maintenance?

With a Browse AI robot

You generally own the robot.

If a website changes enough to break your workflow, you may need to inspect it and retrain or adjust the robot.

Browse AI also promotes automated maintenance capabilities, but do not present this as a guarantee that every website change will be repaired automatically.

The advantage is that Browse AI's visual approach makes manual adjustment possible without necessarily involving a developer.

The disadvantage is equally clear:

you still need a plan for what happens when the workflow stops producing the result you expect.

With a prebuilt Apify Actor

The maintenance model can be different.

If you rely on an Actor maintained by another developer, that developer may update the Actor when the target website changes.

That can remove maintenance work from you.

But it introduces another dependency:

you are relying on the Actor's developer continuing to maintain it.

A developer may stop updating an Actor.

That is why choosing an Actor should not be based only on whether one exists.

A large Actor marketplace doesn't mean a good choice for you

A large marketplace is valuable because there is a better chance that somebody has already solved your problem.

But marketplace size is not the same thing as reliability.

Before building an important workflow around an Actor, inspect it.

Look for signals such as:

  • whether it has been updated recently
  • whether people appear to be actively using it
  • who maintains it
  • whether the documentation is clear
  • what recent users report
  • how its pricing works
  • whether there are credible alternatives if it stops being maintained

A well-maintained Actor for exactly the website you need can make Apify the obvious choice.

An abandoned Actor can do the opposite.

The useful question is therefore not:

“Does Apify have an Actor for this?”

It is:

“Is there an Actor I am comfortable depending on?”

Pricing isn't just price — it's predictability

Comparing advertised entry prices directly gives a misleading picture.

The platforms meter usage differently.

Browse AI

Browse AI uses credits.

Your consumption depends on factors including:

  • rows extracted
  • pages visited
  • screenshots
  • run frequency
  • whether a site receives premium credit treatment

That still requires estimation, but once you understand the workflow, the credit model can make budgeting relatively straightforward.

We built a Browse AI Credits Calculator specifically for estimating this before you schedule a robot.

Premium sites require extra caution because actual consumption can be substantially higher than a standard-site estimate.

Apify

Apify costs are more workload- and Actor-dependent.

Depending on what you run, costs may involve:

  • the Actor's own pricing model
  • compute usage
  • proxy usage
  • storage operations
  • data transfer
  • other platform resources

Actors can use different pricing models, including pay per event, pay per usage, and rental.

Some event-priced Actors include platform usage in their price; others may charge platform usage separately.

That means the headline subscription price is not necessarily the price of your actual workflow.

For an important recurring job, a test run is far more useful than comparing advertised entry prices.

The practical difference

For many solo operators and small teams, the important question is not:

Which platform has the lower starting price?

It is:

Can I predict what this workflow will cost me every month?

If budget predictability matters more than technical flexibility, Browse AI's credit model may be easier to plan around.

If technical flexibility matters more, Apify's more variable cost structure may be an acceptable tradeoff.

Five real workflow decisions

1. I want to monitor competitor prices every morning

Start by testing Browse AI.

A non-technical user can create the extraction workflow, schedule it, and send structured results into a spreadsheet.

If the target site is unusually difficult or a strong source-specific Actor already exists, Apify may still be worth testing.

But this is exactly the kind of recurring workflow where ease of ownership matters.

2. I need a lead list from a specific website

Check whether a suitable Apify Actor already exists — then compare.

If a mature Actor already exists for that source, Apify could give you a working solution without building the extraction logic yourself.

If there is no suitable Actor and the page structure is straightforward, Browse AI may be faster for a non-technical user.

The source matters more than the generic category “lead generation.”

3. I found an Actor built specifically for the platform I need

Apify deserves the first test.

This is one of Apify's strongest advantages.

But don't stop at the Actor's name.

Check its maintenance signals, usage, developer, documentation, pricing model, and recent reliability before depending on it.

4. My workflow lives in Google Sheets and nobody on my team codes

Browse AI is usually the cleaner first test.

The goal is not to build the most technically powerful scraping stack.

The goal is to get useful data into the system your team already uses — and let the team maintain it.

5. A developer is building web data into our product or internal pipeline

Apify is usually the stronger starting point.

Custom Actors, APIs, infrastructure controls, proxy options, storage, and developer tooling matter much more once scraping becomes part of a larger technical system.

At that point, the simplicity advantage of a visual robot may matter less than control.

When Browse AI is the better choice

Browse AI is particularly attractive when:

  • the workflow owner is non-technical
  • you want to build the extraction visually
  • recurring monitoring is central to the job
  • spreadsheets or simple automation tools are the destination
  • predictable planning matters
  • you want the person using the data to maintain the workflow too

Its biggest advantage is not that it can do everything.

It is that you may not need a developer to own the workflow.

When Apify is the better choice

Apify becomes more compelling when:

  • a strong prebuilt Actor already solves your problem
  • developers will own the workflow
  • you need custom scraping logic
  • you need deeper control over browser automation or infrastructure
  • the extraction is part of a larger application or data pipeline
  • technical flexibility matters more than simplicity

Its biggest advantage is not merely scale.

It is the range of ways the workflow can be built and controlled.

When using both makes more sense

You do not necessarily have to standardize on one platform.

Consider a small team that:

  • monitors several competitor pages every day
  • sends those results into Google Sheets
  • occasionally needs a specialized extraction from a difficult platform

Browse AI could own the recurring monitoring workflow.

Apify could handle the specialized job when a suitable Actor or custom implementation makes more sense.

This avoids forcing every web-data problem into the same tool.

For some teams, the right architecture is not:

Browse AI or Apify

but:

Browse AI for simple recurring workflows + Apify for specialized technical jobs.

Before you pay, test the workflow

Both platforms currently provide ways to start without immediately committing to a paid subscription.

Browse AI currently offers a Free plan with 50 credits per month.

Apify currently offers a free platform tier and usage allowance; verify the current amount and terms on Apify's official pricing page before you rely on it.

If you are genuinely undecided, the most reliable comparison is often simple:

run one small version of the same real workflow on both platforms.

Compare:

  • setup time
  • output quality
  • maintenance burden
  • actual usage cost
  • whether the person who will own the workflow can comfortably change it

That test tells you more than another feature table.

FAQ

Is Apify better than Browse AI?

Not universally.

Apify generally offers more technical flexibility and a large marketplace of prebuilt Actors.

Browse AI generally makes it easier for non-technical users to create and maintain recurring extraction and monitoring workflows.

The better choice depends on who owns the workflow and how much technical control it requires.

Does Apify require coding?

No.

You can run existing Actors without building your own Actor from scratch.

However, Apify also provides much deeper developer capabilities when you need custom logic.

The experience therefore depends heavily on which Actor or implementation you use.

Can Browse AI handle large workloads?

Yes.

Large volume alone is not a reason to automatically choose Apify.

Browse AI supports bulk workflows, while Apify also supports substantial workloads.

Compare the actual workflow, site difficulty, maintenance model, and cost rather than using URL count alone.

Which is easier for non-technical users?

Browse AI is generally the easier starting point when a non-technical person needs to build and maintain the workflow.

Apify can also be simple when a suitable prebuilt Actor exists, but the overall platform provides considerably more technical depth.

Which is cheaper: Browse AI or Apify?

There is no universal answer.

Browse AI uses a credit-based model, while Apify costs can depend on the Actor and resources used.

The most useful comparison is the estimated cost of your actual workflow, not the advertised entry price of each platform.

Pricing, platform limits, Actor availability, and product features can change.

Before paying for either service, verify the current terms on the provider's official website and test your actual workflow where possible.
